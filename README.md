# Inclusive Corporate Events

Practical guidance for designing, organising, producing, managing and evaluating corporate events that disabled people can choose, access and participate in with dignity, autonomy and meaningful inclusion.

Use these resources for in-person, remote and hybrid conferences, meetings, launches, awards, workshops, networking and associated hospitality. Include attendees, speakers, staff, contractors, suppliers, volunteers and presenters in planning, not just the final audience.

Accessibility is part of event quality from the first design decision to the next event's improvements. Entering the building, watching a livestream or completing a checklist does not establish that disabled people can participate with dignity and choice. Access and inclusion must be handled through the event lifecycle and verified against actual configured delivery.

| LC-05 | Presentations, activities, networking and live operations |
| LC-06 | Departure, outputs, evaluation and organisational learning |

Stages overlap. Plan evaluation before delivery, receive and update requests throughout, and revisit earlier evidence when a venue, supplier, asset, platform or activity changes. The checklist maps to the lifecycle and should be used to assess the actual configured event rather than a generic concept of accessibility.

## Put the guidance into practice

1. **Define the outcomes.** Establish the event purpose, activities, participation modes and relevant roles. Involve disabled contributors early, name an accountable event owner and access lead, and define meaningful participation outcomes for each mode.
2. **Specify and procure.** Select applicable checklist IDs and explain exclusions. Ask suppliers for current evidence for the actual rooms, facilities, services and configuration. Record any conditions, assumptions or restrictions that affect access.
3. **Inform and coordinate.** Verify accessible invitations, complete booking journeys and equivalent assisted routes. Handle requests privately with owners, response deadlines and confirmed arrangements.
4. **Produce and rehearse.** Review final assets, stage and facility access, service quality, staff coverage, participation methods and failure recovery. Check the occupied layout, configured platform and live-support model before opening.
5. **Deliver meaningful participation.** Monitor sessions, transitions, meals, social activity and remote interaction. Enable Q&A, voting and collaborative tasks through accessible alternatives, with equivalent choices and adequate time for participation.
6. **Evaluate and improve.** Verify accessible outputs, offer the anonymous post-event survey and alternative feedback, examine every lifecycle stage and relevant mode or role, preserve serious individual findings, and carry unresolved barriers forward into the next event.

## Core delivery requirements

- **Connected physical and digital journeys:** registration, travel and arrival, orientation, rooms, stage and backstage, bathrooms, catering, rest spaces, platform controls, materials, departure and follow-up.
- **Communication and presentation:** appropriate captions, interpretation, listening support and visual description; speaker identification; and spoken presentation whose essential meaning is conveyed without relying on a single mode.
- **Accessible participation:** Q&A, polls, voting, apps, round tables and workshops with equivalent alternatives. Allow time for signed-language interpretation, processing and augmentative or alternative communication, not just the nominal session time.
- **Privacy with effective access:** proportionate rules for smart glasses, recording devices, AI note-takers and remote assistance. Address speaker restrictions and privacy-sensitive areas privately and proportionately.
- **Accountable evidence and recovery:** distinguish reported, planned, confirmed and verified provision. Missing evidence is not a pass; accepting a risk does not turn a barrier into a resolved one. Name who is responsible for each action and review point.
- **Evaluation beyond attendance:** offer in-person, remote and mixed-mode attendees the guide's eight-question anonymous survey: participation mode, optional self-selected functional needs, travel and arrival experience, content communication, participation barriers, support quality, and recommendations for improvement.

Autonomy includes choosing and controlling assistance. Do not require unsupported participation, routine diagnosis disclosure or a single communication method. Functional needs may overlap and need more than one form of support.

### Applying and maintaining these resources

The 22 linked documents above were retrieved successfully on 31 October 2026 from 21 public Double-FW repositories. This was a focused relevance and link review, not a fresh audit of every repository or every external reference.

Use the event checklist as the governing catalogue for this event workflow. Related resources explain methods and can reveal additional needs; they do not automatically replace agreed arrangements or formal obligations.

Only Double-FW GitHub resources are linked in this related-resources section. Some supporting documents contain their own external references; those are outside this section's link scope. The Corporate Event Accessibility Guide and the checklist remain the primary event-specific references for this repository.

## Contributing and maintaining the document set

Keep the four core documents aligned when changing stages, checks or methods. Preserve existing checklist IDs; add new IDs for new controls, update the mapping and count, and refresh AGENTS.md's authority and review guidance when the operating model changes.

Review relevant guidance with disabled contributors and delivery specialists. Keep technical conformance, operational delivery, participant experience and legal applicability distinct. Record uncertainties, assumptions and decisions clearly so evidence and responsibility remain auditable.
