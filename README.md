# Inclusive Corporate Events

Practical guidance for designing, organising, producing, managing and evaluating corporate events that disabled people can choose, access and participate in with dignity, autonomy and meaningful influence.

Use these resources for **in-person, remote and hybrid** conferences, meetings, launches, awards, workshops, networking and associated hospitality. Include attendees, speakers, staff, contractors, companions and support workers throughout the journey.

Accessibility is part of event quality from the first design decision to the next event's improvements. Entering the building, watching a livestream or completing a checklist does not establish that someone could understand the content, use facilities, contribute, make choices or obtain effective support.

Stages overlap. Plan evaluation before delivery, receive and update requests throughout, and revisit earlier evidence when a venue, supplier, asset, platform or activity changes. The checklist maps every check to its applicable stages, roles, modes and verification points.

## Put the guidance into practice

1. **Define the outcomes.** Establish the event purpose, activities, participation modes and relevant roles. Involve disabled contributors early, name an accountable event owner and access lead, and fund delivery, testing, support and contingencies.
2. **Specify and procure.** Select applicable checklist IDs and explain exclusions. Ask suppliers for current evidence for the actual rooms, facilities, services and configuration. Record any conditional acceptance, outstanding tests, remedies and decision owners.
3. **Inform and coordinate.** Verify accessible invitations, complete booking journeys and equivalent assisted routes. Handle requests privately with owners, response deadlines and confirmed arrangements. Provide essential suitability information before booking where needed and detailed joining instructions at least 14 days beforehand; apply the documented process for short-notice events, late registrations and corrections.
4. **Produce and rehearse.** Review final assets, stage and facility access, service quality, staff coverage, participation methods and failure recovery. Check the occupied layout and configured platform. Reassess late changes before use.
5. **Deliver meaningful participation.** Monitor sessions, transitions, meals, social activity and remote interaction. Enable Q&A, voting and collaborative tasks through accessible alternatives, with trained facilitators, sufficient response time and chosen support.
6. **Evaluate and improve.** Verify accessible outputs, offer the anonymous post-event survey and alternative feedback, examine every lifecycle stage and relevant mode/role, preserve serious individual findings and verify accepted remedies before closure or supplier reuse.

## Core delivery requirements

- **Connected physical and digital journeys:** registration, travel and arrival, orientation, rooms, stage/backstage, bathrooms, catering, rest spaces, platform controls, materials, departure and re-entry.
- **Communication and presentation:** appropriate captions, interpretation, listening support and visual description; speaker identification; and a spoken presentation whose essential meaning does not depend on seeing slides.
- **Accessible participation:** Q&A, polls, voting, apps, round tables and workshops with equivalent alternatives. Allow time for signed-language interpretation, processing and Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC). Adapt visual and physical demands, and offer agreed support-worker or buddy/team arrangements without compulsory pairing or token roles.
- **Privacy with effective access:** proportionate rules for smart glasses, recording devices, AI note-takers and remote assistance. Address speaker restrictions and privacy-sensitive areas privately, distinguishing capture, processing, transmission, storage and publication. Review current ethical guidance before each event and after material changes.
- **Accountable evidence and recovery:** distinguish reported, planned, confirmed and verified provision. Missing evidence is not a pass; accepting a risk does not turn a barrier into one. Name who can pause, adapt, relocate or delay an affected activity when essential access fails.
- **Evaluation beyond attendance:** offer in-person, remote and mixed-mode attendees the guide's **eight-question anonymous survey**: participation mode, optional self-selected functional needs, five accessible satisfaction ratings and one optional text box. All answers are voluntary. Verify anonymity and protect small groups; survey satisfaction does not replace full lifecycle evaluation or justify ignoring exclusion.

Autonomy includes choosing and controlling assistance. Do not require unsupported participation, routine diagnosis disclosure or a single communication method. Functional needs may overlap and need not correspond to a diagnostic label.

### Applying and maintaining these resources

The 22 linked documents above were retrieved successfully on **31 October 2026** from **21 public Double-FW repositories**. This was a focused relevance and link review, not a fresh audit of every claim or onward reference. Links use current default-branch paths so readers can find maintained material; record the revision actually used for an event and recheck before reuse.

Use the event checklist as the governing catalogue for this event workflow. Related resources explain methods and can reveal additional needs; they do not automatically replace agreed arrangements, determine legal applicability or authorise data collection, supplier commitments or access-denial decisions. Resolve conflicts through the event owner and access lead, with privacy, safety or specialist input where appropriate. Do not import fixed dimensions, universal schedules, software versions or service thresholds without verifying their context.

Only Double-FW GitHub resources are linked in this related-resources section. Some supporting documents contain their own external references; those are outside this section's link scope. The Corporate Event Accessibility Guide separately records its reviewed external source basis for matters such as current wearable ethics and the functional-needs survey framework.

## Contributing and maintaining the document set

Keep the four core documents aligned when changing stages, checks or methods. Preserve existing checklist IDs; add new IDs for new controls, update the mapping and count, and refresh AGENTS.md's reviewed checklist version and content reference. Record the evidence, scope, owner, review date and reason for substantive changes.

Review relevant guidance with disabled contributors and delivery specialists. Keep technical conformance, operational delivery, participant experience and legal applicability distinct. Record uncertainty and retain minority or contradictory findings rather than averaging away exclusion.
