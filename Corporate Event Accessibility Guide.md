# Corporate Event Accessibility Guide

## Purpose, scope and relationship to companion documents

This guide explains approaches and methods for designing, commissioning, producing, managing and evaluating disability-inclusive corporate events. Apply it to in-person, remote and hybrid conferences, meetings, launches, awards, workshops and associated hospitality. Include disabled attendees, speakers, staff, contractors, companions and support workers, with relevant intersecting needs such as dietary requirements, caring responsibilities and time zones. It is not a comprehensive framework for every dimension of corporate inclusion.

The [lifecycle](Disabled-Attendee-Corporate-Event-Lifecycle.md) defines the stages, outcomes, responsibilities and handovers. This guide explains how to achieve them. The [detailed checklist](Inclusive_Live_Event_Venue_and_Delivery_Checklist.md) is the canonical catalogue of verifiable controls; its IDs below identify related checks. [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) defines agent behaviour, evidence, privacy and escalation. Use the documents together; neither this guide nor a completed checklist certifies universal accessibility.

In these documents, **must** identifies a control required by this framework when applicable; **should** identifies a recommended method, and **may** describes an option. Record whether each event requirement arises from a verified legal duty, contract/organisational commitment, individual arrangement or recommendation. Framework instructions are not automatically legal duties. Competent responsible humans determine applicable law and safety arrangements.

Autonomy means control over participation and support, including a person's chosen assistance. The goal is meaningful contribution with dignity and reasonable effort, not unsupported performance. Do not require people to disclose a diagnosis, negotiate separately with every supplier or discover predictable barriers during the event.

## LC-00: Purpose, inclusive design, commissioning and procurement

### Design backwards from participation outcomes

Define what the event is intended to enable: understanding a proposal, influencing a decision, learning a skill, presenting work or making professional connections. List the activities through which people obtain that value and the physical, sensory, communication, digital and processing demands of each activity. Ask what happens if a person cannot see a screen, hear speech, speak, manipulate materials, stand or respond quickly. Design alternative ways to achieve the same purpose before selecting tools or locations.

For a workshop, the outcome might be that each person can understand the task, contribute an idea, influence prioritisation and recognise their contribution in the result. Attendance, watching a stream or having someone else complete the task does not establish that outcome. Decide which activities are interactive in each offered mode and disclose any differences.

Involve relevant disabled contributors early, budget for expertise where appropriate and test assumptions with them. Use walkthroughs and role-based scenarios; do not ask one person to represent every disability or require their participation as a condition of receiving access.

### Specify, enquire and accept in stages

Name an accountable event owner and access lead, with venue, production, communications, registration, facilitation, safety and privacy responsibilities. Fund communication services, accessible assets, testing, support, contingencies and evaluation. Set deadlines from actual supplier lead times and the advertised activities rather than an arbitrary universal schedule.

Use the checklist's specification template: outcome and scope, relevant IDs, required provision and capacity, acceptance evidence, responsible people and deadlines, change/failure arrangements and attendee information. Send equivalent enquiries to suppliers. Ask for actual rooms, routes, assets and platform configurations, not a generic access policy.

Before booking, verify critical suitability through available inspections, demonstrations and relevant evidence. If the final event setup cannot yet exist, identify exactly what remains conditional: owner, final test date, remedy, contingency and contractual change/cancellation protection. A booking approval is a decision under stated conditions; it is not a PASS for untested delivery. Never average away an essential barrier with unrelated successful checks.

**Related checks:** GOV-01–10 and relevant facilities, platform, content and safety checks.

## LC-01: Awareness, invitation and event information

Publish event purpose, audience, costs, timings, location, modes and participation opportunities in structured, readable text. Use meaningful headings, descriptive links, suitable contrast and alternatives for meaningful images. Check emails, websites, invitations and promotional media in their delivered form. Essential information must not depend solely on an image, attachment, QR code or app.

Describe actual entrances, routes, bathrooms, seating, sensory conditions, quiet spaces, catering, communication support and limitations. Explain whether remote attendees can ask questions, vote, collaborate and network. State support-person arrangements before purchase. Provide more than one enquiry channel and realistic staffed response times.

Only confirmed provision may be described as available. A plan can be published as a clearly labelled plan, with its uncertainty, owner and next update. Unknown or unavailable provision must remain visible; do not suggest someone can rely on a service still awaiting confirmation.

Promotional audiovisual content needs the access formats appropriate to its information: accurate synchronised captions for relevant speech and sounds, and description of essential visual information where needed. A transcript may supplement this access; it does not automatically substitute for captions, visual description or signed language.

**Related checks:** TKT-01–03, TKT-06–09, ORI-05–08, CON-04 and OPS-11.

## LC-02: Registration, ticketing and access requests

### Test complete tasks and alternatives

Apply the repository's WCAG 2.2 AA registration baseline to the actual end-to-end process: account creation, sign-in, ticket/session selection, payment, errors, confirmation, changes and cancellation. Combine relevant automated findings with manual keyboard, focus, screen-reader, magnification/reflow, mobile and relevant voice-control checks. Include embedded third-party steps, authentication, time limits and expiry recovery. Record the tested environment and limitations; a checker result alone is insufficient.

Offer online, email and telephone routes with equivalent terms, including human assistance that does not add fees or disadvantage booking priority. Test that published contacts are reachable, appropriately staffed and able to complete the task privately. A telephone-only alternative excludes some people; a form-only feedback route has the same problem. Do not assume an alternative works because its address or number is printed.

### Coordinate requests without unnecessary disclosure

Ask what enables participation through non-exhaustive options and free text. No diagnosis or medical justification is routinely required. Proportionate questions about the activity, support and whether an arrangement will work are appropriate; ask privately and explain why. Invite requirements for speakers, staff and support people as well as attendees.

Explain what will be collected, its recipients, purpose and retention. Keep access information separate from public lists, sponsors and marketing. Use an approved restricted system; share only minimum operational instructions with delivery staff. Do not copy medical narratives into run sheets or infer needs from behaviour or appearance.

Set event-specific acknowledgement, review and confirmation deadlines, staffed hours, urgent routes and overdue escalation. Acknowledge a request, name its owner and next update, agree provision with the person and confirm the supplier and delivery owner. Permit updates and late requests without silent rejection. Distinguish received, confirmed, delivered and verified-effective arrangements; do not count acknowledgement as fulfilment.

**Related checks:** TKT-04–09, REQ-01–07, DIG-01–03.

## LC-03: Preparation, production and pre-event information

### Information that enables preparation

Issue detailed accessible joining instructions at least 14 days before the event. Provide essential suitability information earlier, before booking where needed. For short-notice events or late registrations, issue verified essentials immediately, record the timing exception and impact, identify missing information and assign the next update. A missed date is not automatically an exemption from useful preparation.

Include schedule and breaks, session formats, travel assumptions, entrance and security, accessible route descriptions, rooms, toilets, quiet/rest/prayer arrangements, meals, sensory conditions, leaving/re-entry, communication services, participation methods, help contacts and emergency information. Give remote requirements, a joining test, caption/interpreter controls and low-bandwidth/phone options where feasible. Version the pack and communicate material changes accessibly.

Maps should include ordered text directions, decision points, landmarks, floor connections, known distances and conditions. Keep signs, tickets, room names and agendas consistent. A tactile cue, digital map or staff guide can supplement other information but must not become an untested sole route.

### Physical facilities and hospitality

Inspect the continuous route through entrances, doors, lifts, rooms, stage, backstage, toilets, catering, work areas and exits. Assess applicable building requirements, real layout and equipment, reach, force, turning and transfer needs. A width, access symbol or photograph alone does not establish suitability. Test doors/controls and lifts; reassess with stands, furniture, cables and queues present.

Check bathrooms are available, unlocked, signed, clean and free of storage. Examine transfer arrangements, seated reach, fittings, assistance equipment and response. Where relevant, verify Changing Places availability, route and restrictions; a nearby facility is not automatically a practical substitute. Discuss unmet needs privately before commitment.

Distribute flexible accessible seating with companions and useful views of speakers, captions and interpreters. Review light, glare, flicker, sound, temperature, crowding and smells under realistic conditions. Provide a genuine quiet/recovery space and, where feasible, separate prayer/rest/medication provision. Explain purpose and access without making isolation the only way to rest.

Ask the caterer to confirm requested meals, ingredients/allergens and service arrangements. Verify how relevant cross-contact risks, substitutions, labelling and handover are managed by competent catering staff. An uncertain answer remains unresolved. Review reach, queues, drinking water, seating and quieter eating choices across all hospitality. Offer help carrying food without making assistance compulsory. Confirm assistance-dog water and a usable relief route throughout relevant hours.

### Production, service quality and change control

Maintain an asset/service inventory identifying each session, room/mode, owner, current version, permissions, review result and delivery deadline. Include slides, video, polls, handouts, demonstrations, caption terminology, interpreter preparation and technical feeds. Accessible templates help, but review final exports and the actual platform configuration.

Agree acceptance methods for each communication service with competent providers and reviewers. Consider caption meaning, accuracy, speaker/sound information, useful delay, readability and recovery; interpretation coverage and visibility; listening-system intelligibility and usable controls; and whether visual description conveys essential meaning. Record observed scope and affected activities. Do not invent a universal pass percentage or use caption checks to validate interpretation.

Rehearse sound, cameras, screens, caption feeds, interpretation, breakouts, voting and fallback methods. Create coverage and relief schedules for early joining, sessions, transitions, breaks, hospitality, overruns and departure wherever services are promised. Give temporary staff the same essential operational briefing as permanent staff.

After production approval, a changed slide, speaker, activity, room, feed or platform must trigger review of affected checks and information. Mark assets as approved, pending or withdrawn and ensure the running order references the current version. Where review cannot be completed, the human lead decides an accessible alternative or delay; do not silently use unreviewed content.

**Related checks:** ORI-01–08; BLD, WC, CAT, ENV and DIG; CON-01–12; OPS-01–05, OPS-09–15.

## LC-04: Arrival, orientation and joining

Walk the occupied arrival chain from verified parking/drop-off and transport connections to the correct entrance, security, check-in and every relevant space. Check opening hours, accessible entrance dignity, queues and clear routes. Give optional staffed orientation, seating at check-in, badge/lanyard alternatives and more than one help channel. Ask before assisting and respect the answer.

Open the remote room early, explain joining, captions, interpretation, chat, questions, reactions, support and participation choices, and test the actual controls. Give remote participants a private support route that is itself accessible. Connect room and online contributions rather than making remote people dependent on an unmonitored chat.

Check temporary leaving and re-entry for both modes: doors/security, badges, reconnecting, session links and restoration of captions/interpretation. People should be able to rest, attend to personal needs and return without public explanation or losing their place unnecessarily.

Record the opening check, unresolved barriers and accountable human decision. For emergency procedures, competent venue/safety leads assess alerts, escape, equipment, staffing and individual assistance privately. A refuge alone is not a complete evacuation plan, and AI cannot independently approve one.

**Related checks:** BLD-01–08, ORI, ENV, WC-01–04, DIG-01–08, OPS-01–05 and OPS-09–15.

## LC-05: Presentations, activities, networking and live operations

### Presentations understandable without seeing slides

Brief every speaker to give their name and role. Chairs identify speaker changes when voices may be unfamiliar; avoid unnecessary repetition when identity is already clear. Explain session format and participation routes at the beginning.

The essential argument, evidence and conclusions must be understandable without seeing the slides. Either build a complete spoken narrative that slides support, or integrate explanations of the purpose and relevant meaning of essential images, charts and demonstrations. For example, describe that a trend rises and what that means, rather than merely identifying coloured lines. Read essential quotations, figures or instructions when they are not otherwise spoken. Decorative images do not need exhaustive description.

Rehearse by reviewing the spoken presentation without viewing the slides. For visual demonstrations or spatial material that cannot be adequately conveyed through the planned narration, arrange additional description, accessible materials or an equivalent activity. Keep microphones, captions, interpretation and accessible assets alongside the narrative; a script is not a substitute for those services. Repeat or relay audience questions with permission where anonymity matters, and describe meaningful silent audience responses.

### Q&A, voting and collaborative tools

Explain methods before the activity and provide spoken and accessible written instructions. Offer equivalent ways to ask questions, contribute, vote and receive results without depending on a smartphone, inaccessible app, speech, handwriting, sight, standing or rapid reaction. Test the real voting and Q&A configuration, including anonymous submissions and assisted routes. Provide the same deadline, decision weight and privacy for alternative votes; agree assistance that preserves the voter's choice and secrecy where required.

Use orderly turn-taking and microphones. Allow time for interpretation, processing, typing and AAC composition before moving on; do not privilege only the fastest contribution. Accept speech, signed language, AAC, text, writing or an agreed supporter. Relay contributions to both room and remote participants and to captioning/interpretation. Permit private question submission, observing, declining and breaks without spotlighting someone.

### Facilitation, AAC and supported teamwork

Give facilitators at least basic awareness training before delivery: respectful assistance, communication differences, turn-taking, processing time, AAC, working with interpreters, hybrid moderation, privacy and escalation. Brief them on their actual activity and practise its alternatives. More complex communication arrangements require suitable expertise, not merely an attendance certificate.

Address the participant directly. Wait for an AAC user to complete their message; do not finish sentences, operate their device or paraphrase their contribution without invitation. Make space, power/charging and suitable positioning available where agreed. Ask privately about useful pacing and how a person wishes to indicate a turn or interruption.

Signed languages are languages. A sign-language-first participant may require an appropriate interpreter; written English or a well-intentioned buddy is not automatically equivalent. Arrange sightlines, turn-taking, preparation materials and time for interpretation, and communicate with the participant rather than treating the interpreter as the attendee.

Audit physical and visual workshop demands before approving the task. Adapt materials, tools, instructions, workspace and roles so each person can influence the outcome. Offer a support worker or agreed buddy/team arrangement where useful, with the participant retaining control. Do not impose pairing, allocate only token roles or leave someone watching another person do the task. A buddy does not replace specialist interpretation or agreed professional support.

### Monitor and recover

Keep advertised breaks and start/finish times, explaining changes with practical directions. Monitor communication access, facilities, transitions, catering, exhibition areas and social activities as well as the main session. Preserve remote questions and connect them to the room; do not dismiss access reports because only one person is affected.

Before delivery, agree who can pause, delay, adapt, relocate or cancel an affected activity when essential access fails and no effective alternative is available. Triggers may include loss of a necessary caption feed, usable bathroom, lift, interpreter or joining route. Staff acknowledge the barrier, alert the lead, communicate accessible options and verify the remedy. Seek the affected person's input without making them responsible for the decision. Owner risk acceptance never changes a failed check into PASS.

**Related checks:** CON-01–12; DIG-04–08; CAT and ENV; OPS-01–15.

## Assistive technology, recording and privacy across the lifecycle

### A proportionate event policy

At LC-00 appoint access and privacy leads to agree the policy; discuss speaker requirements during LC-03, publish practical rules before attendance and brief staff before LC-04. Apply rules to functions and contexts, including smart glasses, phones, cameras, AI note-takers, transcription bots and remote assistance, rather than assuming every wearable is recording or every recording-capable device should be banned.

Distinguish capture, local processing, stored recording, live transmission to a remote helper, cloud/AI processing and later publication. Absence of a saved video does not establish that no data leaves a device. Verify relevant settings and supplier information without demanding public disclosure of disability or unnecessary access to personal accounts. Never promise that all devices are offline or non-recording without evidence.

Invite speakers to request no photography, recording, transcription or livestreaming for specified material or sessions. Resolve conflicts privately with the access lead, privacy lead, speaker and affected participant where appropriate. Possible arrangements include approved personal assistive processing, an accessible script, organiser-provided access or a restricted alternative session. Confirm that the arrangement is actually effective; a speaker request is not an automatic authority to exclude essential assistive use. Organiser recording, captions, remote delivery and participant recording may need different agreements.

For toilets, changing areas, personal care and confidential support conversations, prevent capture or transmission of other people's private activity and provide accessible privacy notices. Agree a practical private-use mode or suitable alternative support where someone relies on the technology; do not simply remove their access. Include private breakout/confidential discussion rules and, for remote sessions, platform permissions, bot admission and screen/audio sharing. Do not rely solely on a recording light, visual sign or QR code to explain privacy.

If concerns arise, staff ask discreetly about the function in use and involve the responsible leads. Do not accuse someone based on device appearance, confiscate equipment under this guidance or require medical proof. Follow approved incident procedures if misuse is evidenced; retain minimum necessary details and communicate the outcome privately.

### Keep ethical guidance current

Review before each event and after material device, platform, regulatory guidance, speaker-policy or incident changes. Record the policy owner, date, jurisdiction, sources inspected, settings assumptions, decisions and next review. Consult current regulator guidance and disabled people's organisations rather than treating this edition as permanently current. Review is a human management duty; this document does not perform automatic future updates.

Current source basis, checked 31 October 2026: RNIB's 14 September 2026 position recognises assistive benefits and cautions against exclusionary blanket bans; the ICO's 17 September 2026 discussion emphasises meaningful transparency and privacy-conscious use. The procedures above operationalise those principles for events and are framework recommendations, not quotations or a claim of a settled universal device policy. See resources below.

**Related checks:** GOV-08, REQ-05, CON-06, OPS-02, OPS-11, REV-02 and REV-10.

## LC-06: Departure, outputs, evaluation and organisational learning

### Departure and publication

Keep exit routes, transport information and support available through the actual finish and foreseeable overruns. Explain changes and avoid abruptly disconnecting remote people. Verify captions and interpretation remain available for promised closing contributions. Check recording permissions, consent/privacy arrangements and accessible final assets before publication. Review and correct live captions before publishing prerecorded media; provide transcripts and accessible slides as appropriate, with a contact for inaccessible or missing outputs.

### Anonymous post-event satisfaction survey

The organiser must offer the survey to in-person and remote attendees, including people who used both modes. Response and all questions must be optional. Plan ownership, publication timing, closing date, accessibility testing, anonymity safeguards and reporting before the event. Use a general untracked link rather than a response link associated with a booking. Offer alternative accessible feedback, explaining honestly if a conversation or email cannot be anonymous. A separate contact route handles requests needing individual follow-up; never add an email field to the anonymous response.

Keep the survey to **eight questions total**, including classification and free text. Do not add hidden subquestions or mandatory comments beneath ratings. This concise survey complements, rather than replaces, complete lifecycle evaluation from tests, operations and optional reports.

| No. | Exact survey question | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How did you take part in this event? | In person; remotely; both; prefer not to answer. |
| 2 | Which of the following access needs were relevant to your participation? Select all that apply. | Functional options below; no listed needs; another need not listed; prefer not to answer. No diagnosis requested. |
| 3 | How satisfied were you with the information and arrangements provided before the event? | Five-point satisfaction scale. |
| 4 | How satisfied were you with the venue or online environment you used? | Five-point satisfaction scale. |
| 5 | How satisfied were you with your access to presentations and communication? | Five-point satisfaction scale. |
| 6 | How satisfied were you with your opportunities to contribute and interact? | Five-point satisfaction scale. |
| 7 | Overall, how satisfied were you with your experience of the event? | Five-point satisfaction scale. |
| 8 | What helped, created a barrier, or should change? You may comment on registration, travel, toilets, food, technology, support, privacy, workshops or any other part of the experience. Please avoid names or details that could identify you or someone else. | Optional labelled multiline text box. |

For questions 3–7 use: **Very dissatisfied; Dissatisfied; Neither satisfied nor dissatisfied; Satisfied; Very satisfied**. Add separate **Not applicable/did not use** and **Prefer not to answer** choices; do not score them as neutral. Every question may also be left unanswered. A skipped question is missing data, not dissatisfaction or satisfaction.

### Functional needs options for question 2

The following plain-language adaptations use the structure of EAA Annex I, Section VII(a–j). They are self-selected overlapping functional needs, not diagnoses, eligibility classes or a complete account of disability. The framework's privacy criterion, VII(k), applies across the survey and event, rather than identifying a separate disability group. This use does not establish that an event falls within the EAA's legal scope.

| Source criterion | Participant-facing option |
| --- | --- |
| VII(a) | Access without relying on sight. |
| VII(b) | Support for limited sight. |
| VII(c) | Information that does not rely on distinguishing colours. |
| VII(d) | Access without relying on hearing. |
| VII(e) | Support for limited hearing. |
| VII(f) | Ways to contribute without speaking. |
| VII(g) | Support for limited dexterity or strength. |
| VII(h) | Facilities or controls usable with limited reach. |
| VII(i) | Avoidance of flashing that could trigger seizures. |
| VII(j) | Support for understanding, remembering or processing information. |

Allow more than one functional option; make 'no listed needs' and 'prefer not to answer' exclusive of the other options, with clear accessible instructions. 'Another need not listed' does not require an additional text response; question 8 remains the only comment box. Never infer categories from a booking, device, diagnosis, support person or comment. A person may participate without selecting any group.

### Accessible delivery and anonymity safeguards

Use a clear page structure, native labelled controls, semantic fieldsets/legends for groups, large usable targets and visible focus. Present each rating as its own radio-button group; avoid a dense matrix, slider-only control, star-only labels or colour-only meaning. Label the text box and explain any reasonable character limit. Test keyboard, screen-reader, magnification/reflow, mobile, voice-input operation and submission/error/success feedback. Avoid unnecessary time limits and inaccessible CAPTCHA. Do not require an account or answers before submission.

The privacy lead must verify the actual collection chain: login state, personalised links, IP/device logs, cookies, analytics, timestamps, provider access and exports. Do not describe a survey as anonymous if responses can reasonably be linked to an individual. Disable or separate unnecessary identifiers and document residual processing honestly. If anonymity cannot be supported, fix the method before launching it as the required anonymous survey; other confidential feedback can still proceed with accurate notices.

Before sharing results, assess identifiability in small events and rare combinations of needs and mode. Suppress or combine unsafe breakdowns, redact identifying comments and restrict raw responses. Do not publish tiny groups simply because names were omitted. Set context-specific reporting safeguards; no universal minimum cell count guarantees anonymity. Preserve the underlying barrier for action without identifying the respondent.

### Evaluate the whole lifecycle

Use the checklist's evaluation dimensions and every lifecycle stage. Combine technical results, operational observations and voluntary experience reports while keeping their evidential roles distinct. Review arrival, toilets, catering, support, privacy, departure and role-specific experiences even though the short survey cannot assign a separate rating to everything. Ask additional questions only through a separately designed optional research activity, not hidden extra questions in this eight-question survey.

Report rating distributions and response denominators, including missing/not-applicable answers, rather than relying only on an average. Where safe, compare in-person, remote and mixed-mode experiences and self-selected functional groups; acknowledge overlap, small samples and non-response. Survey responses do not represent everyone or prove causal effects. Do not use a majority satisfaction result to dismiss an individual barrier.

Review booking abandonment and people unable to attend without assuming disability or reasons. Offer a distinct optional route for those perspectives; do not describe non-attendees as attendee survey respondents. Assign accepted improvements, owners, deadlines and verification methods, update future briefs and supplier decisions, and communicate changes where appropriate.

Keep separate retention rules for personal requests, restricted incidents, raw survey data and suitably de-identified operational learning. Apply approved access and deletion schedules, preserving unresolved supplier/configuration barriers without carrying forward unnecessary individual details.

**Related checks:** BLD-09, GOV-09, REV-01–10 and TKT-09.
