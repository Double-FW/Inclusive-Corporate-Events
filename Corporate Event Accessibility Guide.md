# Corporate Event Accessibility Guide  
  
Planning an accessible corporate event requires moving beyond basic legal compliance to create an environment where all participants can engage equally.   

## Pre-Event & Registration

*   **Early Outreach:** Include a clear access statement on invitations. Ask what would enable people to take part, and invite dietary, mobility, sensory or communication requests without requiring a diagnosis or explanation.
*   **Accessible Registration:** Ensure your registration platform meets **WCAG 2.2 AA** standards and offer multiple registration methods (online, email, phone) to accommodate different needs.
*   **Information Sharing:** Send detailed "Joining Instructions" at least 14 days in advance. Include an **accessible map** showing step-free routes, quiet rooms, and accessible restrooms. The map should work without colour, be high contrast and come with a text-only alternative that describes how to find things and how to request assistance.
*   **Timing:** Choose timings with travel, caring responsibilities, fatigue, medication, time zones and breaks in mind. There is no single time window that works for everyone; publish the schedule and travel assumptions early.
*   **Communicate this information to attendees and presenters.** Invite people to share anything that would enable them to participate, including stage access, support-person arrangements, assistance-dog facilities, toilet access or emergency evacuation support. Explain how requests will be handled privately and who to contact.

## Venue & Physical Access

*   **Entrances & Corridors:** Check the full route to every event space, including entrances, lifts, stages, toilets and catering. Confirm clear widths and turning space against applicable building requirements, the venue layout and attendee needs; a single stated width does not establish that a route is accessible. Keep routes free of furniture, cables and queues.
*   **Seating Layout:** Do not relegate wheelchair users to the back. Integrate accessible seating throughout the room, including near exits and the stage.
*   **Toilets:** Ensure accessible toilets are available, unlocked, signed and not used for storage. Check the facilities and transfer arrangements rather than assuming all accessible toilets meet every person's needs. Where appropriate for the event and venue, identify nearby Changing Places facilities and explain how to reach them.
*   **Rest and reflection spaces:** Provide a quiet or low-stimulation space and, where feasible, a separate space for prayer, rest or medication. Explain what each space is for, where it is and how to access it; do not make a quiet room the only place where someone can take a break.

## Content & Communication

*   **Presentations:** Brief speakers to describe visual elements on slides where they represent key content.
*   Check videos for accurate captions, meaningful sound information and a description of important visual information where needed. Avoid flashing content that could trigger photosensitive reactions; provide warnings and alternatives where relevant.
*   At the beginning of panel discussions, invite panellists to introduce themselves by name and role. Describe the format and explain how audience members can identify speakers and contribute.
*   **Communication access:** Plan captioning, sign language interpretation, hearing support and other communication access from attendee and presenter requests, event format and the communication needs of the audience. State what is available, confirm it with suppliers, and test it in every room and online session. Offer suitable sight-lines and seating without isolating attendees.
*   **Digital materials:** Share agendas and accessible presentation materials in advance where possible, and provide them promptly afterwards. Budget for accessible formats, captioning, transcripts and other access services as part of event production; do not make attendees' access dependent on a presenter personally funding or producing these. Respect copyright and agree permissions and delivery formats early.
*   **Ensure all workshops and activities are accessible.** If in doubt, involve an event accessibility specialist. This includes ensuring digital as well as physical collaborative spaces and activities are accessible.
*   **Remote and hybrid viewing:** Frame speakers clearly, provide reliable sound and captions, and keep interpreters visible when used. Camera framing can support some lip-readers but does not replace captions or other communication access.

## Catering & Hospitality

*   **Inclusive menus:** Label food clearly with ingredients and allergens. Ask about dietary needs and plan varied options, including vegan, gluten-free, halal and kosher choices where requested and feasible. Do not assume these labels cover every dietary requirement.
*   **Accessible setup:** Check table height, reach, circulation space and queueing with the venue. Offer help carrying trays without making assistance compulsory.
*   **Assistance dogs:** Confirm the venue's arrangements for assistance dogs, including water and an appropriate outdoor relief route. Share practical information with attendees and staff.

## Staffing & Training

*   **Staff preparation:** Brief all on-site staff on respectful assistance, access arrangements, routes, communication support, incident reporting and the venue's emergency procedures. Agree individual evacuation support privately with the person and venue; do not assume a generic plan fits everyone.
*   **Point of contact:** Assign a named access lead and make their contact details available before and during the event. Train greeters to offer help and ask before providing it.

## Post-Event

*   **Feedback:** Offer an accessible, optional survey and alternative feedback routes. Ask about barriers and participation, explain how feedback will be used, and protect personal information.
*   **Follow-up:** Share recordings with accurate captions and transcripts where consent, privacy and recording permissions allow. Provide accessible slides and materials, and a contact for reporting access problems.
