# Inclusive Live Events: Venue and Delivery Review Checklist

For organisers, venue and production teams, procurement staff and AI agents reviewing specifications, preparing enquiries, comparing offers, arranging authorised bookings and providing feedback.

## Purpose and intended outcome

Check the complete event journey, from discovering and booking an event to travelling home and receiving its materials. Cover attendees, presenters, staff, contractors, companions and support workers. Apply the relevant checks to in-person, remote and hybrid participation, including workshops, exhibitions, networking, meals and associated social activities.

The intended outcome is participation with choice, independence, dignity and comparable access to information and interaction. Reaching the main room is only one part of that outcome. A person must also be able to use essential facilities, communicate, contribute, obtain support and leave.

This document operationalises the public Double-FW guidance listed in the source register. Stable IDs, status codes, decision gates and record templates are checklist design choices derived from that guidance; they are not additional laws or a certification scheme. Do not describe an event as universally or “fully accessible” because this checklist has been completed.

## How to use the checklist

1. **Specify:** define the event, participation outcomes, audience, locations, rooms, activities and delivery responsibilities. Include access in the budget and supplier brief from the start.
2. **Enquire:** send the applicable checks to suppliers. Ask for current, event-specific evidence, limitations and proposed remedies.
3. **Verify and decide:** inspect the actual configuration and complete journeys. Record unresolved barriers and obtain decisions from the accountable human owner before making commitments or public claims.
4. **Confirm and deliver:** put agreed provision into booking documentation, rehearse, check before opening and monitor throughout.
5. **Review:** obtain optional accessible feedback, verify remedial actions and update information before the next booking.

Tick a box only when its expected outcome is verified for the recorded scope. An empty box can mean not assessed, pending or a barrier; always use the status record to distinguish these. A supplier's statement alone is not a verified pass. An unanswered question remains unknown.

### Status and evidence

| Code | Meaning | Recording rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NA | Not assessed | No conclusion; name an assessor and date. |
| ENQ | Enquiry or confirmation pending | Identify the question, supplier and response deadline. |
| PLAN | Planned provision | Record the delivery owner and dependency; do not advertise it as confirmed. |
| CONF | Provision confirmed | Retain written confirmation; practical verification is still required. |
| PASS | Works as intended in the assessed scope | Record method, evidence, date, configuration and limitations. |
| BAR | Barrier found or required provision unavailable | Record impact, remedy, owner, deadline, contingency and decision. |
| NAPP | Not applicable | Explain why; identify who accepted the scope decision. |

Keep evidence types distinct: **supplier-reported**, **document inspected**, **measured/observed**, **functionally tested**, and **attendee or presenter experience**. For example, a supplier's photograph can show a toilet layout but does not establish that an alarm is working. A past inspection does not establish that today's route is clear.

For each item, classify its event-specific basis as **legal/regulatory**, **contractual/organisational**, **attendee-specific arrangement**, or **recommended practice**. Record the authority for any claimed duty. The checklist itself does not establish legal applicability. Physical dimensions and safety arrangements require assessment against the applicable jurisdiction, building, layout, equipment and individual needs; do not substitute a universal door width or generic evacuation plan.

## 1. Event specification, ownership and procurement

**Outcome:** access is a funded delivery requirement with named owners, evidence and remedies. **Sources:** S1–S3, S4, S10.

- [ ] **GOV-01 — Define the whole journey.** List every activity and location, including arrival, security, check-in, auditorium, stage, breakout rooms, exhibition, meals, social events, rest spaces, toilets and departure; include equivalent remote tasks where offered.
- [ ] **GOV-02 — Assign authority.** Name the event owner and access lead, and allocate responsibilities across venue, communications, registration, production, catering, security, presenters and platform suppliers. Give staff a clear escalation route.
- [ ] **GOV-03 — Fund access early.** Include accessible materials, captioning, interpretation, hearing support, individual arrangements, testing and contingencies in the brief, programme and budget. Do not depend on presenters personally funding access work.
- [ ] **GOV-04 — Involve disabled people.** Include relevant lived-experience expertise in planning, walkthroughs, testing and review; budget for paid contributions where appropriate. Do not make attendees responsible for discovering or repairing predictable barriers.
- [ ] **GOV-05 — Require supplier evidence.** Request dated plans, access information, measurements where relevant, photographs, demonstrations and test results for the proposed rooms and configuration. Record gaps, restrictions and temporary works.
- [ ] **GOV-06 — Contract for delivery.** Put agreed access provision, quantities/capacity where relevant, locations, staffing, setup, rehearsal, reporting and remediation responsibilities into supplier briefs and booking documentation.
- [ ] **GOV-07 — Check dependencies and terms.** Confirm access-service availability, technical requirements, named contacts, delivery deadlines, cancellation/change terms and workable fallbacks before commitments are made.
- [ ] **GOV-08 — Review changes.** Require notice of room, layout, supplier, platform or programme changes. Reassess affected checks and update attendees' information when access changes materially.

## 2. Event information, invitation and ticketing journey

**Outcome:** people can assess suitability, register and manage their booking without avoidable barriers or disclosure. **Sources:** S1–S3, S5, S8, S10.

- [ ] **TKT-01 — Describe the offer accurately.** State format, location, costs, timings, participation choices and which parts are available live or later. Explain whether remote attendees can contribute, network and join breakouts, or only watch.
- [ ] **TKT-02 — Publish concrete access information.** Describe routes, toilets, seating, sensory conditions, quiet spaces, food, communication access and limitations. Distinguish confirmed, planned and unavailable provision; avoid blanket access claims.
- [ ] **TKT-03 — Check invitations and promotion.** Use meaningful headings, logical reading order, descriptive links, readable contrast and text alternatives. Keep essential details available as text and caption promotional audiovisual content appropriately.
- [ ] **TKT-04 — Test the complete booking process.** Include account creation/sign-in, ticket and session selection, access/support-person options, payment, errors, confirmation, booking changes and cancellation. Check embedded and third-party steps too.
- [ ] **TKT-05 — Verify digital operation.** Test keyboard use, visible and predictable focus, labels, instructions, error correction, screen-reader operation, magnification/reflow, mobile use and relevant voice-control interaction. Check timing, authentication and session-expiry barriers. Record actual environments and results.
- [ ] **TKT-06 — Offer equivalent registration help.** Provide online, email and phone options and a human-assisted route with equivalent terms. Explain contacts and response times; a phone-only fallback is insufficient.
- [ ] **TKT-07 — Explain companion arrangements.** Make support-worker/companion registration, seating, access and any applicable no-additional-charge arrangement clear before purchase. Resolve ambiguity about costs or entry conditions with the owner.
- [ ] **TKT-08 — Make confirmation usable.** Provide readable confirmation and instructions for check-in, changes and cancellation. Ensure people can retrieve essential booking information without depending solely on an image, attachment, QR code or inaccessible app.

## 3. Access requests, support coordination and privacy

**Outcome:** requests are handled discreetly, owned and delivered without repeated explanation. **Sources:** S1–S3, S10.

- [ ] **REQ-01 — Ask about participation.** Invite dietary, mobility, sensory, communication, presenter and other requirements through useful non-exhaustive options and free text. Ask what will enable participation; do not routinely require a diagnosis or medical narrative.
- [ ] **REQ-02 — Confirm request status.** Acknowledge each request, name its owner, distinguish confirmed arrangements from open issues and give a follow-up date. A received request is not a fulfilled request.
- [ ] **REQ-03 — Allow updates and late requests.** Explain how requests can be changed and how late requests will be considered. Route unresolved or conflicting requirements to the access lead rather than silently rejecting or delaying them.
- [ ] **REQ-04 — Coordinate across suppliers.** Translate agreed needs into minimum operational instructions. Avoid making the person negotiate separately with venue, catering, production and platform teams or repeatedly provide the same information.
- [ ] **REQ-05 — Protect sensitive information.** Explain purpose, recipients and retention; collect the minimum necessary; restrict access by delivery need. Separate requests from public attendee lists, marketing, sponsors and general analytics.
- [ ] **REQ-06 — Verify the arrangement with the person.** Check privately that the proposed support addresses the relevant activity, including temporary or fluctuating needs. Ask before assisting and respect a person's choice.

## 4. Travel, arrival and building access

**Outcome:** attendees and presenters can reach all required spaces through usable, predictable routes. **Sources:** S1–S4; S7 for route information.

- [ ] **BLD-01 — Check the arrival chain.** Verify the route from relevant public transport, parking and drop-off to the correct entrance. Record known distance, steps, gradients, surfaces and route restrictions; distinguish verified details from unknowns.
- [ ] **BLD-02 — Check actual entrance availability.** Confirm opening times, security procedures and the usable accessible entrance during setup, event hours and departure. Provide an equally dignified route through gates or turnstiles.
- [ ] **BLD-03 — Inspect continuous routes.** Walk through entrances, corridors, lifts, all event rooms, toilets, catering, rest spaces and exits. Assess clear door openings, thresholds, turning/manoeuvring space, door forces and controls for actual users and equipment.
- [ ] **BLD-04 — Test doors and controls.** Check that doors can be identified, approached and operated with relevant seated reach, strength and dexterity. Check control position and door swing; do not infer usability from width alone.
- [ ] **BLD-05 — Test lifts.** Confirm the lift serves every required floor and accommodates relevant equipment. Check reachable controls, tactile information where appropriate, visual and audible floor/direction information, operating hours and failure contingency.
- [ ] **BLD-06 — Check non-visual route safety.** Identify glazing, changes of level, stairs, handrails and decision points through appropriate contrast, lighting and cues. Avoid glare and confusing patterns; use competent design for tactile guiding surfaces.
- [ ] **BLD-07 — Check the occupied layout.** Reinspect routes with furniture, AV equipment, stands, cables, catering and queues in place. Check busy periods and transitions, not just an empty building.
- [ ] **BLD-08 — Include presenter and staff routes.** Verify access to stage, rehearsal, green room, backstage and work areas relevant to each role. Check lectern, table and microphone choices; do not substitute an accessible audience seat for stage access.
- [ ] **BLD-09 — Arrange departure.** Confirm accessible exit routes, transport information and a help contact remain available at the advertised finish and after overruns. Include linked accommodation routes where accommodation forms part of the event.

## 5. Orientation, wayfinding and advance information

**Outcome:** people can plan and navigate without being required to use a particular sense, device or assistance. **Sources:** S1–S4, S6, S7.

- [ ] **ORI-01 — Send joining instructions on time.** Issue detailed accessible joining instructions at least **14 days before the event**, as specified in the Corporate Event Accessibility Guide. If that point has already passed, record the gap, issue verified information promptly and escalate missing essentials.
- [ ] **ORI-02 — Provide usable maps and directions.** Show entrance, floors, lifts, step-free routes, rooms, toilets, quiet spaces, help points and exits. Supply an accessible text route with ordered decisions, distances where known, landmarks and relevant conditions.
- [ ] **ORI-03 — Make signs consistent and findable.** Match room names and numbering across tickets, agendas, maps, booking systems and physical signs. Use legible, high-contrast text, symbols and arrows at decision points; do not rely on colour alone.
- [ ] **ORI-04 — Support non-visual orientation.** Provide appropriate tactile information and reachable, predictable signs where useful. Offer staffed familiarisation or orientation when wanted. Apps, QR codes and navigation technology must supplement other information.
- [ ] **ORI-05 — Explain what to expect.** Describe security, check-in, queues, badges, room layouts, participation formats, transitions, food, lighting, likely noise, smells, crowding and quieter options.
- [ ] **ORI-06 — Publish a realistic schedule.** Include start/finish times, breaks and travel assumptions. Consider fatigue, medication, caring responsibilities and time zones without claiming one timetable suits everyone.
- [ ] **ORI-07 — Keep information current.** Date/version the pack and provide a correction/contact route. Recheck routes and facilities after changes and tell attendees about material differences before arrival where possible.
- [ ] **ORI-08 — Keep essential information available.** Check phone usability and accessible web/document editions. Provide alternatives to image-only maps, inaccessible PDFs, QR-only instructions and phone-only help.

## 6. Bathrooms and personal facilities

**Outcome:** people can identify and use suitable facilities throughout the event. **Sources:** S1–S4.

- [ ] **WC-01 — Confirm location and availability.** Check accessible toilets on the actual event route, opening times, signs, doors and any access procedure. Keep them unlocked, available, clean and free of storage.
- [ ] **WC-02 — Inspect the layout.** Record approach, manoeuvring and transfer space, transfer side/arrangement, grab supports and usable controls against relevant guidance and expressed needs. A toilet's access symbol is not evidence of suitability for every person.
- [ ] **WC-03 — Check reach and operation.** Inspect toilet paper, washbasin, taps, soap, drying facilities and other essentials for usable seated/short-stature reach and limited dexterity. Identify barriers requiring a different facility or arrangement.
- [ ] **WC-04 — Verify assistance arrangements.** Check any emergency assistance equipment and the response process with the venue, including reach where relevant. Confirm who responds and that the arrangement remains operational during the event.
- [ ] **WC-05 — Identify additional provision.** Where appropriate to the event and venue, identify Changing Places facilities, whether on site or nearby. Confirm availability and explain the route, restrictions and practical implications; do not treat a nearby facility as automatically adequate.
- [ ] **WC-06 — Communicate suitability privately.** Give factual facility information early and offer a confidential discussion of unmet needs. Escalate unresolved toilet access before the booking decision; do not expect someone to discover the limitation on arrival.

## 7. Catering and hospitality

**Outcome:** attendees can obtain suitable food and drink and participate socially with dignity. **Sources:** S1–S4.

- [ ] **CAT-01 — Confirm dietary provision.** Ask what is needed and confirm suitable options with the caterer, including vegan, gluten-free, halal or kosher choices where requested and feasible. Do not assume menu labels cover every requirement.
- [ ] **CAT-02 — Make food information reliable.** Require clear ingredient and allergen information and staff who can answer questions discreetly. Verify how a specific request will be delivered rather than treating an unanswered enquiry as confirmation.
- [ ] **CAT-03 — Inspect the serving arrangement.** Check table height, reach, circulation, queues and access to drinking water and essential items in the final layout. Assess the actual service process as well as the furniture.
- [ ] **CAT-04 — Offer seating and assistance choices.** Provide usable tables and seating for meals and refreshments. Offer tray-carrying or other help without making it compulsory; avoid standing-only hospitality.
- [ ] **CAT-05 — Provide quieter participation.** Offer a quieter eating/social option and access to breaks. Preserve a choice to join others rather than making isolation the default arrangement.
- [ ] **CAT-06 — Include all hospitality.** Check refreshments, receptions, dinners, exhibition catering and social venues, not just the main meal. Communicate any limitations before people choose to attend.
- [ ] **CAT-07 — Plan for assistance dogs.** Confirm water and an appropriate outdoor relief route; explain arrangements to attendees and staff. Keep the route usable during all relevant event hours.

## 8. Room layout, sensory environment and rest

**Outcome:** the occupied environment supports varied mobility, sensory and communication needs. **Sources:** S1–S4.

- [ ] **ENV-01 — Integrate seating choices.** Distribute accessible seating within the audience, including useful positions near exits and stage. Keep companion spaces flexible and preserve access to other participants.
- [ ] **ENV-02 — Verify sightlines and sound.** Check views of speakers, screens, captions and interpreters from relevant seating. Check audibility and hearing support without isolating people who need it.
- [ ] **ENV-03 — Assess sensory conditions.** Review glare, flicker, reflections, abrupt lighting changes, background noise, acoustics, temperature, crowding and smells during realistic operation. Identify control options and quieter routes/spaces.
- [ ] **ENV-04 — Provide low-stimulation space.** Check location, route, availability, noise and purpose of a genuine quiet/recovery space. Explain how to use it; it must not become storage or an overflow meeting room.
- [ ] **ENV-05 — Provide rest choices.** Where feasible, provide a separate prayer, rest or medication space and explain its purpose. Do not make the quiet room the only place someone can take a break.
- [ ] **ENV-06 — Make check-in manageable.** Provide seating, ways to reduce or avoid queues, alternatives where badges/lanyards create barriers and respectful staffed orientation. Do not make assistance compulsory.

## 9. Event platform, remote participation and hybrid operation

**Outcome:** remote participants can receive information, contribute and obtain help in the actual event setup. **Sources:** S1–S3, S8, S9.

- [ ] **DIG-01 — Explain technical requirements.** State platform, supported device/browser requirements, login process, captions, interpretation, participation methods and support. Offer a joining test and phone/low-bandwidth options where feasible.
- [ ] **DIG-02 — Test the configured journey.** Check invitation links, sign-in, joining, session navigation, breakout rooms, shared content, chat, questions, polls, support and leaving. Do not accept a platform's general claim in place of event-specific testing.
- [ ] **DIG-03 — Verify access controls.** Test keyboard and screen-reader operation, focus, status announcements, magnification and relevant display preferences. Include actual caption controls, interpreter view and interactive content in the test record.
- [ ] **DIG-04 — Rehearse communication delivery.** Verify audio feeds, captions, interpretation, framing, lighting and shared materials in every relevant room/session. Make captions readable and interpreters visible when used; a good camera view does not replace other communication access.
- [ ] **DIG-05 — Connect room and remote participants.** Test microphones, cameras and moderation so both groups can hear contributions and ask questions. Assign a host to bring remote questions into the room and room questions online.
- [ ] **DIG-06 — Include participation beyond the main stream.** Check breakouts, collaboration, workshops, networking and breaks. State limits before ticket selection; do not label passive viewing as equivalent interaction.
- [ ] **DIG-07 — Provide live technical help.** Open the room early, explain controls and provide a co-host or private support contact. Ensure moderation retains access-related questions and allows different ways to contribute.
- [ ] **DIG-08 — Rehearse failure recovery.** Test what happens if a feed, access service or connection fails. Confirm the owner, communication route and practical fallback; do not leave an affected attendee to troubleshoot alone.

## 10. Content, presenters and communication access

**Outcome:** people can follow and contribute to the programme through appropriate, dependable formats. **Sources:** S1–S3, S6–S9.

- [ ] **CON-01 — Confirm services against needs.** Plan captions, sign language interpretation, assistive listening, visual description and other support from the audience, format and requests. Record confirmed suppliers, rooms, sessions and technical dependencies.
- [ ] **CON-02 — Brief presenters and hosts.** Cover names/roles, session format, microphones, pace, turn-taking, repeating audience questions, describing meaningful visuals, breaks and access-failure reporting. Do not require public disclosure from attendees.
- [ ] **CON-03 — Prepare accessible materials.** Review structure, reading order, readable type, contrast, meaningful image/chart descriptions and alternatives to colour-only meaning. Check final slides, handouts and exported files, not just source templates.
- [ ] **CON-04 — Check video and motion.** Verify accurate captions, meaningful sound information and description of important visual information where needed. Avoid harmful flashing and unnecessary motion; provide relevant warnings and alternatives. A warning alone does not resolve the barrier.
- [ ] **CON-05 — Check live caption quality.** Supply names, terminology and running orders; provide clean audio. Review accuracy, meaning-changing errors, delay, speaker identification, relevant sound and recovery. Automatic transcription is not verified captioning by default.
- [ ] **CON-06 — Agree materials and permissions early.** Set formats, fees, deadlines, copyright, speaker permissions and recording consent in advance. Share accessible materials before the event where possible and promptly afterwards; avoid QR-only access.
- [ ] **CON-07 — Support presenters privately.** Confirm transport, stage and green-room access, lectern/microphone options, equipment, communication support, rehearsal and running-order changes. Include these in production plans without unnecessary disclosure.
- [ ] **CON-08 — Make activities adaptable.** Check physical and digital workshops, collaborative spaces, demonstrations and audience tasks. Offer routes that do not depend solely on standing, eye contact, rapid responses, speech, dragging or public participation.
- [ ] **CON-09 — Preserve distinct access services.** Do not substitute transcripts for synchronised captions, captions for signed language, a livestream for participation or one generic format for everyone's needs. Resolve proposed substitutions with the access lead and affected person where relevant.

## 11. Staffing, support, emergencies and opening checks

**Outcome:** promised provision works throughout the event and failures receive an accountable response. **Sources:** S1–S4.

- [ ] **OPS-01 — Maintain the contact chain.** Publish the named access lead's contact before and during the event, with more than one way to ask for help. Keep venue, owner, production, captioner/interpreter, platform and safety contacts available to staff.
- [ ] **OPS-02 — Brief every delivery team.** Include greeters, security, temporary staff, volunteers, catering, hosts and technicians. Cover respectful assistance, communication support, assistance dogs, mobility aids, routes, privacy and incident reporting.
- [ ] **OPS-03 — Agree emergency arrangements.** Have the responsible venue/fire-safety lead assess escape routes, relevant audible/visual/vibrating alerts, equipment, staffing and response. Agree individual assistance privately where needed; a refuge alone is not a complete evacuation plan.
- [ ] **OPS-04 — Verify safety ownership and practice.** Confirm competent human approval and staff familiarity/practice for emergency arrangements. AI may coordinate records but must not create or approve a personal evacuation plan or final safety decision independently.
- [ ] **OPS-05 — Conduct a pre-opening check.** Verify clear routes, signs, toilets, quiet spaces, seating, staff cover, accessible materials, microphones, hearing systems, captions, interpretation and the online room. Record failures and actions before doors open.
- [ ] **OPS-06 — Monitor the whole programme.** Check transitions, queues, breaks, social areas, catering and hybrid contributions as well as formal sessions. Keep advertised breaks and timings; announce changes with practical directions.
- [ ] **OPS-07 — Respond to access failures.** Alert the human lead promptly, acknowledge the barrier, explain the response privately where appropriate and arrange a practical alternative. Record impact, owner, resolution and verification.
- [ ] **OPS-08 — Escalate unresolved exclusion.** Refer failed access services, denied/unconfirmed requests, inaccurate information, unsafe treatment, discrimination and privacy exposures to the relevant leads. Do not close an issue merely to protect budget, timetable or reputation.

## 12. Departure, follow-up materials and feedback

**Outcome:** people can leave, use event outputs and influence improvements without being burdened with fixing delivery. **Sources:** S1–S3, S6, S9, S10.

- [ ] **REV-01 — Support departure.** Give clear closing instructions, accessible exit and transport information and a help route. Explain overruns or changed arrangements and avoid abruptly disconnecting remote participants.
- [ ] **REV-02 — Verify final outputs.** Check permissions, consent and privacy before sharing recordings or images. Review and correct live captions before publishing the recording as prerecorded media; provide usable transcripts and accessible materials as appropriate.
- [ ] **REV-03 — Offer accessible feedback choices.** Provide an optional short accessible form and alternatives such as email or private conversation. Explain use, confidentiality, response times and complaints/urgent-concern routes.
- [ ] **REV-04 — Ask about actual participation.** Cover information, booking, arrival, toilets, catering, orientation, platform, content, support, social activity and departure. Ask what was blocked, difficult or helpful, and about independence, effort and confidence; do not require diagnosis.
- [ ] **REV-05 — Include missing voices.** Consider registration abandonment, people who declined after reviewing access information and differences between modes. Do not infer the experiences of non-respondents or treat no complaints/high satisfaction as proof of access.
- [ ] **REV-06 — Preserve minority findings.** Investigate serious barriers even when reported by one person. Separate observation from interpretation and summarise without exposing individuals or identifiable small groups.
- [ ] **REV-07 — Close the improvement loop.** Review with relevant disabled contributors and suppliers, assign actions and dates, verify remedies and update future briefs. Tell attendees what changed where appropriate and apply the approved retention/deletion policy.

## Decision gates for specification reviews and bookings

These gates implement the source guidance on evidence, escalation and human accountability. They do not authorise spending, sending enquiries or making bookings by themselves.

| Point | Evidence needed | Handling an unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specification ready for enquiry | Defined journeys, outcomes, owners, budget, applicable requirements and selected checklist IDs | Identify omissions before asking suppliers to price or confirm the brief. |
| Venue/platform shortlisted | Current evidence for the proposed rooms/configuration; known limitations and inspection/test plan | Mark unknowns as pending; compare barriers and remedies rather than ranking a vague accessibility claim. |
| Booking decision | Written commitments, access-service confirmation, delivery owners, tested critical dependencies, terms and contingencies | Escalate a material gap to the event owner/access lead before commitment. Record the decision and any conditions; do not silently accept a participation barrier. |
| Public access information issued | Confirmed provision with precise scope, limitations, contacts and current joining instructions | Correct inaccurate claims; label planned services honestly. |
| Event opens | Pre-opening verification and operational support/fallbacks | Human lead decides the response to outstanding barriers and communicates practical options. |
| Issue closed / supplier reused | Remedy delivered and verified; feedback and residual limitations recorded | Carry unresolved findings into the next specification; do not reset them to pass. |

## Creating an event specification

Use the checklist to define outcomes before selecting a venue or accepting a supplier's proposed solution. A specification should identify what people need to do, the provision that supports it and the evidence required to accept delivery. Do not issue a brief that merely asks for an “accessible venue” or “accessible platform”. **Sources:** S1–S4, S8–S10.

### Minimum specification structure

For each event location, service or activity, include:

1. **Scope and intended outcome:** who uses it, in which participation modes, and what they must be able to accomplish. Include presenters and staff where relevant.
2. **Applicable checklist IDs and requirement basis:** identify the relevant checks and distinguish organisational commitments, agreed individual arrangements, recommendations and verified legal duties.
3. **Required provision:** describe the route, facility, format, equipment, staffing or support needed. Identify capacity, operating hours and dependencies where they affect delivery.
4. **Acceptance evidence and method:** state the documentation, inspection, measurement, demonstration, functional test or experience review required, including the actual room, layout, version and configuration.
5. **Ownership and deadlines:** identify the supplying party, delivery owner, verifying reviewer and human decision-maker; set enquiry, confirmation, rehearsal and correction dates.
6. **Failure and change arrangements:** require prompt notification of limitations, substitutions and outages, with a named response owner and contingency. Recheck affected participation outcomes before accepting changes.
7. **Communication and privacy:** specify what attendees need to know, when it will be provided and which operational details require restricted access.

### Example specification clauses and acceptance evidence

The following examples translate source guidance into procurement language. Adapt them to the event and agreed needs; they do not introduce universal dimensional or service-quality thresholds.

| Area and IDs | Example specification clause | Evidence for acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Building journey: BLD-01–08 | Identify and maintain a continuous usable route from arrival through all booked facilities and presenter destinations during the relevant operating hours. Disclose restrictions and lift dependencies. | Dated route plan, relevant measurements and a walkthrough of the final setup; test doors, controls and lifts; record limitations and failure arrangements. |
| Bathrooms: WC-01–06 | Identify the facilities available to the event, their transfer arrangements, usable controls, assistance arrangements and operating hours. Keep them available and clear. | Layout information and inspection; functional check of relevant assistance equipment and response process; confirmation against agreed needs. |
| Catering: CAT-01–07 | Confirm requested dietary provision, reliable ingredient/allergen information, usable service arrangements and seated/quieter participation options. | Written catering confirmation, final service-layout review, menu/label review and staff briefing. Unconfirmed requests remain open. |
| Ticketing: TKT-04–08 | Meet the Corporate Event Accessibility Guide's WCAG 2.2 AA registration baseline for the actual booking journey and provide equivalent human-assisted registration. | Automated findings plus manual and relevant assistive-technology tests through account, selection, payment, confirmation, changes and cancellation; record defects and retests. |
| Platform: DIG-01–08 | Enable the advertised remote activities, communication access and support in the configured event. Declare differences between room and remote participation. | Rehearsal of joining, controls, captions/interpreter view, questions, breakouts and recovery, with named hosts and technical owners. |
| Content and services: CON-01–09 | Deliver agreed accessible materials and communication services for the specified sessions, with usable sightlines/audio and rehearsed dependencies. | Reviewed final assets; supplier confirmation; production rehearsal; caption-quality review and a monitoring/failure-response plan. |
| Support: REQ and OPS | Provide a named access lead, discreet request handling, trained staff, confirmed arrangements and an accountable incident response. | Restricted operational request record, role/contact list, staff briefing and documented escalation and response arrangements. |

### Reviewing supplier responses

Compare suppliers against the same journeys and outcomes. For every response, identify the evidence date, the precise scope and whether the response concerns existing provision or a future promise. Ask follow-up questions about missing information rather than treating silence as acceptance.

Record differences as participation consequences: for example, a longer step-free route, a toilet with an unsuitable transfer arrangement or a remote mode that cannot join workshops. Identify remedies, costs, owners and contingencies for human review. Do not combine unrelated passes into a score that conceals an essential barrier.

## Managing delivery through the event lifecycle

Use the following schedule as an operating sequence. Except for the source's 14-day joining-instruction requirement, dates must be set for the actual event and supplier lead times. **Sources:** S1–S3.

| Management point | Required activity | Accountable handover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brief and initial planning | Define outcomes, modes, owners, budget, requirements and lived-experience input; select checks and evidence methods. | Event owner approves the brief; access lead coordinates delivery responsibilities. |
| Before booking | Resolve supplier enquiries, inspect/test critical dependencies, confirm provision and record terms, limitations and contingencies. | Event owner receives the decision record and unresolved access risks before commitment. |
| Registration opens | Verify public information, ticketing and request channels; put privacy controls and response ownership in place. | Communications and registration leads hand confirmed commitments to delivery teams. |
| At least 14 days before the event | Issue verified joining instructions with schedule, travel, routes, facilities, sensory information, communication access and support contacts. | Access lead checks consistency and owns corrections to material access information. |
| Before rehearsal and production lock | Obtain presenter requirements and permissions; prepare materials, terminology, technical feeds, staff instructions and service-provider confirmations. | Production, venue and platform owners demonstrate readiness and identify outstanding dependencies. |
| Rehearsal and before doors open | Test the actual room/platform configuration, final content, routes, facilities, support and failure arrangements. | Named reviewers record results; the human lead decides responses to outstanding barriers. |
| During delivery | Monitor access services, transitions, social activities and remote interaction; maintain breaks, contacts and private incident handling. | Staff escalate promptly; the access lead coordinates remedies and records outcomes. |
| After delivery | Verify outputs and permissions, offer feedback, review incidents and assign improvements. | Event owner tracks accepted actions through verification and updates the next brief. |

Maintain one live action record across these handovers. Every open issue needs an owner, due date and next decision point. Keep confirmed operational arrangements available to authorised delivery staff, including temporary staff, without copying personal narratives into general run sheets.

## Evaluating the event and providing feedback

Evaluation should establish whether people could complete the activities they chose and whether access worked reliably. Technical checks, operational evidence and attendee experience answer different questions; retain all three. **Sources:** S2, S3, S6, S10.

### Evaluation dimensions

| Dimension | Review question | Evidence to examine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information and choice | Could people judge suitability and prepare using accurate information? | Access-statement accuracy, joining-instruction timing, unresolved enquiries and reported surprises. |
| Registration and requests | Could people book, update requests and receive confirmed arrangements without repeated explanation? | Journey tests, completion/abandonment information where proportionate, response and fulfilment records, feedback. |
| Facilities and movement | Could people reach and use the rooms, stage, bathrooms, catering and rest facilities they needed? | Final-layout inspections, incident records, observed obstructions and attendee/presenter reports. |
| Content and communication | Could people follow the programme and identify speakers, meaning and relevant visual/audio information? | Material reviews, caption/interpretation delivery, quality issues, failures and reported comprehension barriers. |
| Participation and parity | Could people ask questions, collaborate, network and participate in their chosen mode? | Moderation records, workshop/breakout checks and feedback separated by participation mode where privacy permits. |
| Autonomy and dignity | Could people participate without compulsory assistance, public disclosure, stigma or disproportionate effort? | Optional reports of independence, confidence, effort, fatigue and treatment by staff. |
| Support and resilience | Were problems acknowledged and practical alternatives delivered promptly? | Incident timeline, owner response, contingency delivery, verification and affected-person feedback where available. |
| Organisational learning | Were accepted remedies completed and reflected in future specifications? | Action owners, deadlines, verification records, supplier responses and changes to briefs. |

### Interpreting measures

Set proportionate event-specific measures before delivery. Useful measures from the event guidance include registration completion and abandonment, request response and fulfilment time, caption/interpretation availability and reliability, defects identified and resolved, access incidents, reported ability to participate and completed improvement actions.

Record what each measure covers and what it cannot show. For a request-fulfilment figure, distinguish received, confirmed, delivered, unresolved and unavailable arrangements; do not count acknowledgement as delivery. For a service-reliability figure, state the relevant sessions and observed failures rather than relying only on a supplier's availability claim. Do not set invented universal pass percentages.

Review severe individual barriers alongside aggregate trends. A majority satisfaction score cannot compensate for someone being unable to use a toilet, enter a session or follow its content. Where possible, include insights from people who abandoned booking or declined participation, without profiling disability or assuming why they withdrew.

### Closing the evaluation

Prepare a concise report containing scope and evidence limitations, confirmed strengths, barriers by checklist ID, participation consequences, immediate responses and outstanding actions. Separate supplier evidence, observation, attendee reports and reviewer interpretation. Include accessible response routes and any agreed follow-up to individuals in the appropriate restricted records.

For each accepted improvement, name the human owner, deadline, required evidence and verification method. Record the outcome before closing it. Carry unresolved barriers into future supplier enquiries and booking decisions; retain minority findings and protect confidentiality when sharing feedback.

## Additional operating rules for AI agents

- Identify the event stage, participation mode, responsible human and authorised scope before acting. Draft and analyse within that scope; obtain explicit authority or use an approved workflow before sending enquiries, publishing claims, spending, booking, changing or cancelling commitments.
- Use verified sources and current supplier evidence. Preserve the distinction between reported, planned, confirmed and tested provision. Do not fabricate dimensions, test outcomes, supplier replies or access-service availability.
- Do not infer disability or needs from appearance, speech, behaviour, names or activity data. Never turn operational access records into diagnostic profiles or use them for marketing/sponsor analysis.
- Escalate unknowns affecting participation, rejected or unresolved requests, material changes, unavailable contingencies, access failures, discrimination and privacy incidents. Explain impact and options; name the human decision-maker.
- Do not make final access-denial, eligibility, legal, medical, safety or evacuation decisions. Do not silently replace an agreed service with a different format.
- Keep records proportionate and redacted. If approved access controls or deletion cannot be enforced, stop processing identifiable access information and escalate for an approved alternative.
- Report scope and residual limitations honestly. Automated checks can support digital review; they cannot verify the physical venue, the meaning of content or an individual's ability to participate.
